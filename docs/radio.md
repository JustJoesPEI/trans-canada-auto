# Radio: ads and the voice

The writing for the radio, worked out with Joe one piece at a time. The
humour rules in `docs/design.md` (section 8) still apply; this file is
how they sound in practice. Everything here is approved and ready to record.

## The voice

- **Straight face, honest line.** Nobody in an ad knows they're funny.
  They play it completely sincere, and the true thing slips out near the
  end: what the company really thinks, what the customer won't admit.
- **Grown-up worries, not gags.** Money, houses, marriage, getting older,
  waiting for things, being stuck. The joke is that it's true.
- **Aimed up.** The landlord, the lender, the company, the province, the
  repeat offender. Never the person who's struggling.
- **Make fun of the bad thing, never make it cool.** Drunk drivers are
  the joke, their excuses and the businesses that live off them. No line
  that would work on a T-shirt for the wrong people.
- **Specific and local.** Real Island habits (Saturday at Countess Auto,
  the riding mower, Ontario buyers) beat general jokes every time.
- **Vary the format.** A nature documentary, a phone menu, a press
  conference, a brand film. The format is half the joke.
- **About 30 seconds.** Short lines. The last line is the punchline or
  the tagline, never an explanation.
- **Made-up names only.** World brands from the README's table; no real
  people, parties, officers or businesses.

## Approved

### Countess Auto
*(Hushed, like a nature documentary)*
**NARRATOR:** Saturday morning. 8:58. The Island husband has told his wife he needs one bolt.
He will not find the bolt.
He will find a winch. He has nothing to winch. He'll think of something.
By noon he'll own a pressure washer, forty feet of chain, and a hydraulic cylinder he can only describe as "a deal."
Next Saturday he'll be back. For the bolt.
**ANNOUNCER:** Countess Auto. He'll be home when he's home.

### Island Dream Homes
**AGENT** *(silky)*: Hi, I'm Candace. Thinking of investing on the Island? Of course you are. You're from Ontario.
This charming two-bedroom in Brighton has been in the same family for sixty years. It'll be in yours for about eighteen months, and then it's an Airbnb.
Original hardwood. Original windows. Original tenant, but we're working on that.
**ANNOUNCER** *(fast)*: Every home sold over asking. Every local priced out, lovingly.
**AGENT:** Island Dream Homes. You can't live here, but your money can.

### MedLine Anywhere
**ANNOUNCER:** Still no family doctor? Neither do we. Introducing MedLine Anywhere: a real physician, licensed in a province you've never been to.
**DOCTOR** *(bad connection)*: Can you hold the rash closer to the camera... closer... you're frozen.
**PATIENT:** You're frozen.
**DOCTOR:** It's either viral or it's Tuesday. Take an Advil.
**ANNOUNCER:** MedLine Anywhere. You're still on the list. This just helps you feel like you're not.

### Breathe Easy Interlock
*(Bright, cheerful, a company that's doing very well)*
**ANNOUNCER:** At Breathe Easy, we install ignition interlocks all across the Island. Business has never been better, and we'd like to thank you for that.
**CUSTOMER:** I only had two.
**ANNOUNCER:** They all only had two.
And now there's the Family Plan: one interlock for the truck, the side-by-side, and the riding mower.
**CUSTOMER:** They took the mower too?
**ANNOUNCER:** Breathe Easy Interlock. Our customers are the most loyal in the country. Most of them come back.
*(Quickly, a little nervous)*: Please don't call a cab.

### Breezy Home
*(Cheerful, a showroom voice)*
**ANNOUNCER:** Why wait to own nice things? At Breezy Home, take home a sixty-five-inch TV today for just nineteen ninety-nine a week!
**CUSTOMER:** For how many weeks?
**ANNOUNCER:** Yes!
Couches. Microwaves. Throw pillows. And now, for the first time on the Island: paper towel.
**CUSTOMER:** I'm financing paper towel.
**ANNOUNCER:** Six rolls, three forty-nine a week for a hundred and four weeks. By the time it's paid off, you've used it. That's the Breezy way.
Face cloths: financed. A single fork: financed.
**ANNOUNCER** *(fast)*: TV, four thousand three hundred dollars over the life of the agreement. TV, in the store next door, six hundred.
Breezy Home. Low weekly payments. Forever.

### McGuinness Funeral Home
*(Quiet, kind, an organ in the distance. The director never raises their voice)*
**DIRECTOR:** At McGuinness Funeral Home, we know what it's like on the Island. You wait for everything.
You wait to be big enough for the booster seat. Then tall enough for the go-karts in Cavendish. Then old enough for your licence.
Then you go on the list for a family doctor. You're still on it.
You get sick, and you wait ten hours in Emergency. Then you wait in the hallway, because there's no bed.
Then they find something on the scan, and you wait for the appointment.
*(Beat.)*
Some of us wait a little too long.
But at McGuinness, there's no list. Your bed is ready.
**ANNOUNCER** *(fast, gentle)*: Pre-planning packages are very, very, very expensive. But you won't be waiting. And by then, you won't mind.
**DIRECTOR:** McGuinness Funeral Home. Finally, no wait.

### Early Years, Eventually
*(A hopeful government ad: soft piano, children laughing)*
**ANNOUNCER:** You're starting your life. Getting married. Buying a home. Starting a family.
Well, not yet. First you have to move out of your parents' basement.
And at thirty-six, you finally have! Congratulations on your new home: seven hundred and fifty thousand dollars. Ten years ago it was a hundred and fifty.
But don't worry about that. Because when you're ready for kids, Island child care is just ten dollars a day!
So go ahead. Buy the house. Buy the formula. Live the dream.
**DAD** *(off, loud)*: Ten dollars a day! And they still complain!
**ANNOUNCER:** Sir, this is a paid—
**DAD:** When I was twenty-three I was at the packing plant. I had a house, a wife, two cars and a cottage in Brackley. Paid cash!
**ANNOUNCER:** Spaces are limited—
**DAD:** He's thirty-seven and he's still in my basement!
**ANNOUNCER:** He moved out last year.
**DAD:** He's back. The mortgage went up.
If he'd just stop buying those four-dollar coffees, he'd own the place by now.
**ANNOUNCER** *(fast)*: The average Island home costs about two hundred thousand coffees.
Ten-dollar-a-day child care. Your Province. Early Years, Eventually.

### Election Week (four ads, played back to back)
**BLUE** *(confident, flags in the wind)*: For seven years, the Blues have given the Island steady leadership.
Under one premier. Then another. Then one who left for Ireland. Then one who didn't want the job, then did, so he quit the job to apply for the job, and got the job back.
That's stability. Vote Blue. Ask for your premier by name. Ask quickly.

**RED** *(warm, nostalgic piano)*: Remember the Reds? Remember the good years? The jobs. The immigration program. The loans.
*(Fast)*: Please don't remember the immigration program. Or the loans.
The Reds. We've changed. Our cousins haven't, but we have.

**GREEN** *(recorded in a kitchen, sincere)*: Hi. We're the Greens. Every few years, you remember us for about a week.
This is that week.

**ORANGE** *(one man, too close to the mic, an echo)*: Hi! The Orange Party here! We're running candidates in nine ridings! Well, one candidate. He moves around. Is this on? I paid for this myself.

**ANNOUNCER:** Election day is coming. Whoever wins, the roads get paved by the same family. They've been very generous to all four parties.

### Island Electric
*(An automated phone line, pleasant and endless)*
**VOICE:** Thank you for calling Island Electric, a proud member of the Fortress family of companies.
If your power is out, press 1.
*(Beep.)*
Thank you. Your power is out.
To choose a different power company, press 2.
*(Beep.)*
There is no 2.
To hear why your bill is going up, press 3.
*(Beep.)*
We're upgrading your power lines. To pay for the upgrades, we're raising your rates. When it's done, we'll own the upgrades.
We're also building new oil-burning generators for four hundred and twenty-seven million dollars. That's the future.
About the last big storm, press 4.
*(Beep.)*
The regulator says we should have trimmed the trees. We disagree, and we're taking it to court. You'll be paying for the lawyers too.
Your power comes from New Brunswick, through a cable under the Strait. To speak to someone in New Brunswick, press 5.
*(Beep.)*
They don't want to talk to you either.
If you live in Summerside, you have your own power. Please hang up and check on your solar farm.
**ANNOUNCER:** Island Electric. Choose us, or don't. Same bill.
